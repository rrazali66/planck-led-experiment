Measuring Planck’s Constant from LED Threshold Voltages

Enter each LED’s peak wavelength λ and its measured threshold (turn-on) voltage V₀. The tool fits 1/λ against V₀, extracts h = e/(m c), propagates the uncertainty, and flags the systematic problems that matter more than the answer.

1 · Theory

eV₀ = hν = hc/λ  →  1/λ = (e / hc) · V₀
slope m = e/(hc)  →  h = e / (m · c)
The constant term is not forced to zero. A real diode’s first-glow threshold sits below Eg/e, so the correct model is 1/λ = m·V₀ + b. Any constant offset is absorbed by b and does not bias the gradient — which is exactly why the gradient, never the individual points, must be used.

5 · Constants & method notes

e = 1.602176634×10⁻¹⁹ C  ·  c = 2.99792458×10⁸ m s⁻¹  ·  hCODATA = 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J s
  • Uncertainty on h comes from the standard error of the slope: σh = h · (σm/m). This is the honest precision of the experiment.
  • λ should be the measured peak wavelength (spectrometer), not the datasheet nominal colour.
  • Use the voltage across the LED, not across the LED + series resistor.
  • Thresholds below ~750 nm cannot be found by eye — a photodiode or camera is required.
